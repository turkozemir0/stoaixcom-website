Patient enquiries

First reply templates for patient enquiries: WhatsApp, Instagram and web forms

The first reply is a patient's first real experience of your clinic. This guide breaks it into five parts and gives templates for each channel that you can adapt in your own words.

Published: 20 November 20268 min read

This article is part of the Patient enquiry management: from first message to booked consultation series.

Short answer: A good first reply has five parts: the patient's name, who is writing, context showing why they got in touch, a short but genuine answer to their question and one easy question. Sending a price list, a long introduction to the clinic or just "please book a consultation" does not do the job. The message gets shorter or longer depending on the channel, but the structure stays the same.

What is a first reply, and why treat it separately?

The first reply is the first personal response a patient receives after messaging your clinic or filling in a form. An automatic "we've received your message" does not count; the first reply that addresses their question, uses their name and moves the conversation forward does. It deserves its own attention because this is how the patient meets your clinic. They have not met the clinician, have not visited and may not even have looked at your website; all they have is these few lines. If the message is a pasted price list, they remember you as a price list. If it uses their name, takes the question seriously and ends with one easy question, they feel there is a person on the other side. The aim of a first reply is not to sell but to earn the second message: a reply from the patient, which means a conversation has started. That is also how it should be measured: not by how many bookings it produced, but by how many conversations it started.

The five-part first reply

Build every first reply from these five parts. Whatever the channel, the order stays the same:

PartWhat it doesExample line
1. NameShows the message is not copy-and-paste"Hi Emma,"
2. Who is writingTells them there is a person here"I'm Jess, the treatment coordinator at the practice."
3. ContextShows you know why they got in touch"I saw your question about dental implants."
4. Short answerProves the question was taken seriously"The fee depends on how many implants are needed and on the bone, so we can only give an accurate figure after looking at an X-ray."
5. One questionMoves the conversation one small step on"Do you have a recent X-ray from your dentist?"

The part most often skipped is the fourth. Asking a question without answering theirs feels like an interrogation. The answer can be short, but it must be real.

Templates by channel

These templates are skeletons. Fill in the brackets and keep your clinic's own tone. Names are fictional.

WhatsApp: an enquiry that opens with price

Hi [Name], I'm [Coordinator], the treatment coordinator at [Clinic]. Thanks for your message about [treatment]. The fee depends on [what it depends on, e.g. the number of teeth and bone levels], so the most accurate answer comes after a quick look at your situation. Do you have a recent X-ray or a photo you could share?

On WhatsApp, keep it to one bubble. Voice notes work better once the patient is already chatting, not as the very first message. For how to answer the price question in more depth, see answering patient price questions.

WhatsApp: a general information request

Hi [Name], I'm [Coordinator] from [Clinic]. I saw you'd like to know more about [treatment]. In short: [a two-sentence description with no promised outcome]. So I can point you to the right information: how long have you been thinking about this?

Instagram DM: a reply to a story or post

Hi [Name], thanks for getting in touch, I'm [Coordinator] from the team at [Clinic]. I saw your question about [topic of the post]. [Short answer]. To give you more detail, which is easier for you: carrying on here or switching to WhatsApp?

Instagram can be a little more casual, but it should still match the clinic's general tone. Suggesting a channel change is left to the patient; it is never pushed.

Web form: when the call is not answered

Calling a form enquiry first is a good start. If there is no answer, leave a short message straight away:

Hi [Name], it's [Coordinator] from [Clinic]. You asked about [treatment] on our website and I've just tried to call you. I'm happy to call again at a time that suits you, or you can reply here with any questions. Which would be easier?

Missed call: they rang and nobody picked up

Hi, it's [Coordinator] from [Clinic]. You called us around [time] today and we couldn't get to the phone, sorry about that. Could you let me know here how we can help, or would you like me to call you back at a time that suits?

Out of hours: the assistant's first reply

Hi [Name], thanks for contacting [Clinic]. I'm the clinic's AI assistant, and here is a short answer to your question about [treatment]: [approved short information]. Our treatment coordinator will reply to you here tomorrow morning. In the meantime, may I ask: [one easy question]

It matters that the assistant introduces itself as an assistant. If the patient later realises they were not talking to a person, trust suffers.

What not to do in a first reply

  • Send a price list PDF. It does not answer the question; it sends the patient back to comparing.
  • Open with a long clinic introduction. Paragraphs about years of experience and an expert team get in the way of the patient's question.
  • Promise outcomes. Lines like "you'll have the smile you've always wanted" are outcome claims and can fall foul of the CAP Code when they appear in marketing.
  • Ask several questions at once. Three questions in a row usually get no answer at all.
  • Create false urgency. "Our offer ends this week" damages trust in a medical decision and is the kind of pressure the ASA and professional regulators look at.
  • Speak for the clinician. "The best option for you is…" is the clinician's call, not the coordinator's.

How to measure the first reply

The success of a first reply is measured by one question: did the patient reply? Each week, track enquiries that replied at least once after the first message / enquiries that received a first message, and split it by channel. If one channel is clearly lower, change the template, but change one thing at a time: first the question, then the context line. Wait two or three weeks and look at the effect. How this fits with the other rates is covered in patient enquiry conversion metrics.

Write templates as a team

When only the manager writes the templates, the team either ignores them or rewrites them their own way. A better method is to read five or ten messages from the last month that actually got replies, find what they have in common and build the template from them. That way it is written in the team's own voice.

Sending every first reply at the same speed and quality, especially in the evenings and at weekends, is hard for a person. Frequently asked questions

Should we give a price in the first reply?

Rather than a bare figure, explain what the fee depends on and share an honest range where you can. The patient feels their question has been answered and you keep the chance to give an accurate figure after an assessment. Any range you give should be realistic and not used as a hook.

How long should a first reply be?

On WhatsApp and Instagram, a few sentences in a single message is enough. It should be readable on a phone screen without scrolling. More detail comes in the second and third messages once the patient replies.

What if there is no reply to the first message?

Leave one more trace the same day, on a different channel or at a different time, then move to a planned follow-up calendar. Each follow-up should carry something new. See unresponsive patient enquiries for detail.

Don't templates sound insincere?

A template is a skeleton, not the whole text. If the patient's name, question and context change in every message, they will not notice the template; they will simply feel they are dealing with an organised, responsive clinic. What sounds insincere is not a template but a message that ignores the question.
