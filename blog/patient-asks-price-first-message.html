When a patient asks the price in their first message

A large share of first messages to clinics are a single word: "Price?". How to answer without ending the conversation or pushing the patient back into comparison mode.

Published: 20 November 20264 min read

Short answer: Do not hide the price and do not send a bare number. Give an honest range, say in one sentence what it depends on and ask one easy question. The range has to be real: a "from" price that doubles at the treatment-plan stage destroys more trust than any first message can build.

The two classic mistakes

Dodging. "We can only give a price after a consultation." "Send us photos and we'll give you a personal quote." Patients read this as "it's expensive and they don't want to say". They close the tab and message the clinic that answered.

A bare number. "£2,400." A number with no context becomes a cell in a comparison spreadsheet. If the next clinic says £1,900, you are out, regardless of what was included.

Both mistakes end in the same place: the patient goes back to search mode and a price list makes the decision for them.

The honest-range formula

  1. The range: the low and high end you actually charge.
  2. What it depends on: one or two concrete factors (number of teeth, graft count, materials).
  3. One easy question: something that narrows the range.
  4. Optional: what is included (follow-ups, aftercare, for international patients transfers and accommodation).

Example: "Implant treatment with us is usually between £X and £Y per tooth; the difference comes down to bone condition and the implant system used. If you can tell me how many teeth are involved, I can give you a much narrower range."

What is behind the price question?

IntentSignalWhat to do
Budget check"Roughly how much?"Give the range, ask about options that fit
Testing youOne word, nothing elseAnswer fast and clearly; that is the test
Not knowing what to ask"Can I get info, price?"Range plus "most people also want to know…"
Comparing"Another clinic said…"Compare scope, not numbers

You do not need to memorise this. Just remember that a price question is the start of a conversation, not the end of one.

Answering by channel

  • Public Instagram comments: never post numbers in the open. Reply that you have sent details by DM and move the conversation to private.
  • WhatsApp: the full formula, in three or four short sentences.
  • Phone: say the range, then pause. Their reaction tells you the next question.

Is "we don't quote prices" ever right?

Some clinics deliberately do not quote before an assessment. That is legitimate as a clinic policy, not a salesperson's tactic, and the reason should be stated: "Plans vary so much that even a range can mislead, so we give a firm figure after a free assessment." Without a reason it is just a dodge.

In the UK, price claims in ads and promotional messages fall under the CAP Code: a "from" price must be genuinely available to a meaningful share of patients. Urgency lines such as "half price this month only" invite both complaints and distrust.

What comes after the price?

After the range, the aim is to move toward a personal plan: clinical assessment, an X-ray or photos if needed, then the plan itself. If the patient says "that's expensive", do not panic; it is a comparison, and we cover how to handle it in our guide to the "too expensive" objection.

Frequently asked questions

Can you answer a price question without any number?

Yes, but explain why. "Let's talk after the consultation" with no reason sends most patients to a clinic that gives a number.

What if our range is very wide?

"Between £1,000 and £10,000" is not information. Ask one question that narrows it and give a tighter range in your second message.

What do I say when a patient asks for a discount?

Ask why first. If the budget is genuinely limited, talk about changing the scope rather than the price. Discounts given for nothing weaken every future quote.

What if a competitor is cheaper?

Compare scope, not numbers: materials, number of reviews, what is included, who performs the treatment. Go through it line by line with the patient, without criticising the other clinic.
