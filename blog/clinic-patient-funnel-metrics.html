Clinic patient funnel metrics: where are you losing patients?

"The ads didn't work this month" is usually wrong. The problem is more often in one of the stages between enquiry and treatment. To see it, you need ratios, not gut feeling.

Published: 20 November 20263 min read

Short answer: Split the funnel into written stages (enquiry, first reply, conversation, assessment, quote, deposit, treatment) and calculate one ratio per stage: the number who move on divided by the number at the previous stage. Use medians for times and cohorts for conversion. Compare against your own last-90-day baseline, not invented industry averages. The stage you control that loses the most patients is your first bottleneck: one bottleneck, one focus.

Why funnel metrics

Most clinic owners know two numbers: ad spend and patients treated. Everything in between is a black box, so when a month goes badly, ads get the blame. More often the problem is unanswered enquiries, unsent quotes or patients nobody followed up.

Stages and formulas

Write the stage definitions down. "Quote sent" and "quote discussed" are not the same.

MetricFormulaWhat it shows
First response timeTime from enquiry to first human or assistant reply (median)Speed
Response rateEnquiries answered / all enquiriesLeakage
Conversation rateReal conversations / answeredFirst-message quality
Assessment rateSent photos or records / had a conversationTrust
Quote rateReceived a quote / assessedOperational speed
Quote acceptancePaid a deposit / received a quoteQuote and follow-up quality
Show rateAttended / paid a depositPost-booking communication

Medians and cohorts

Use medians for times, not averages. One enquiry answered two days late skews the average and hides all the fast replies.

Use cohorts for conversion. Comparing this month's enquiries with this month's treatments misleads, because most of this month's patients enquired last month. Track each month's enquiries as a cohort and keep immature cohorts separate.

Your own baseline

Most "industry average" figures online are unsourced or don't fit your market. Build a baseline from your last 90 days and compare with yourself. One external source is worth knowing: a 2011 Harvard Business Review study of 2,241 US companies found that firms responding within an hour were nearly seven times as likely to qualify the lead as those responding an hour later. We explain why speed is the first metric in speed to lead.

Find the bottleneck

Trying to improve everything improves nothing. Ask: where is the biggest loss, and is that stage in my control? Where both answers meet is your first bottleneck. If the response rate is low, polishing the quote template is wasted effort.

The weekly scorecard

Monthly results arrive late; a weekly scorecard catches problems early. Track three layers:

  • Lead activities: enquiries answered, conversations held, quotes sent, follow-ups done.
  • Results: deposits, treatments.
  • Quality: the share of conversations ending with a clear next step.

Take 15 minutes every Friday: what worked, what didn't, what is next week's single focus? Keep the focus for at least two or three weeks; a constantly changing strategy produces no measurable effect.

Frequently asked questions

Which metric should I start with?

First response time and response rate. They are the easiest to measure and, in most clinics, where the biggest losses happen.

Is a spreadsheet enough?

To start, yes, but stage changes must be entered by hand and that usually slips. A CRM that records stages automatically makes measurement sustainable.

How should I record why patients were lost?

Use a short, fixed list: price, timing, trust, another clinic, clinically unsuitable, no response. Free text cannot be analysed.
