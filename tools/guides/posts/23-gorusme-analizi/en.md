> **Short answer:** Get the patient's explicit consent first and store recordings in line with GDPR. Then assess each conversation against a written rubric of 5–7 criteria (opening, needs discovery, handling concerns, clear next step and so on), scoring each 1–5 with a supporting quote. Use AI as a second listener, not a judge: the manager makes the final call and does the coaching. Listening to two conversations a week together changes more than hundreds of automatic scores.

## Why analyse conversations

Funnel metrics tell you where you lose patients; conversations show why. If quote acceptance is low, the reason is usually inside the call: needs weren't explored, price came too early, or the call ended without a clear next step. For the metrics, see [clinic patient funnel metrics](/blog/clinic-patient-funnel-metrics).

## Consent and privacy first

- Say at the start that the call will be recorded and why; don't record without consent.
- Limit access, set a retention period and cover the processing in your privacy notice.
- Health data is special-category data. Check where your AI tool processes it and whether it is used for model training.

## The rubric

| Criterion | Score 1 | Score 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Opening | Straight to price or a brochure | Warm welcome, asks why the patient called |
| Needs discovery | Closed questions, interrogation | Open questions, listens to the patient's story |
| Handling concerns | "Don't worry" | Names the concern, offers proof |
| Accuracy | Vague or wrong information | Clear, clinician-approved information |
| Price conversation | A number before needs are understood | Alongside value and options |
| Close | "Have a think and get back to us" | A clear, dated next step |

Every score needs a quote from the call. A score without evidence turns coaching into an argument.

## AI as a second listener

AI is good at summarising, giving a first score against the rubric and finding quotes. It doesn't always read tone, cultural context or what a patient's silence means. So:

- Treat the AI score as a starting point, not the final mark.
- Don't use scores alone for penalties or bonuses.
- Regularly have the manager and the AI score the same calls separately; if they differ widely, tighten the rubric.

## The weekly routine

1. **Two calls a week:** one that went well, one that was lost.
2. **Listen together:** the coordinator assesses themselves first.
3. **One development topic:** one week needs discovery only, the next week the close only.
4. **An example library:** collect the best moments (consented and anonymised) for new team members to learn from.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is recording calls legal?

It is possible when you inform the patient in advance, obtain consent and process the data in line with GDPR. Check your privacy notice with your legal adviser.

### Can AI assess my coordinator?

It can produce a first score and summary, but the final assessment and coaching should stay with a person.

### How many calls should I analyse?

Automatic summaries can cover every call; for deep review, two calls a week is enough and sustainable.
