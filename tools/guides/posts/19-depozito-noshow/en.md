> **Short answer:** A deposit secures a decision the patient has made and protects the clinician's time; it is not a tool to force a decision they haven't. Ask for it once clinical suitability, a written plan, a date and the decision-makers' agreement are in place. Keep the amount meaningful but not frightening and share a written refund policy before payment. Don't go quiet afterwards: a contact plan counting back from treatment day is the strongest protection against no-shows.

## What a deposit is for

A deposit does two jobs: it makes the patient's decision concrete and it protects the time the clinician and team have set aside. It is not a way to push an undecided patient. Deposits taken that way end in refund requests, complaints or no-shows.

## The right moment

Ask once:

1. The clinician has confirmed suitability.
2. The patient has a written plan and quote.
3. The date has been agreed together.
4. Decision-makers (partner, family) are on board.
5. The patient's last question is answered.

If any step is missing, the request feels like pressure.

## Amount and refund policy

| Topic | Good practice |
|---|---|
| Amount | Meaningful but not frightening; deducted from the total |
| Refund policy | Short, written, shared before payment |
| Clinical unsuitability | The patient is protected if treatment is not appropriate after examination |
| Date changes | Clear terms for free rescheduling and notice periods |
| Payment channel | Official, verifiable channels only |

Never ask for card or ID details by WhatsApp or message. For a patient worried about scams, an official payment link is one of the strongest pieces of proof.

## The first ten minutes after payment

Send a warm confirmation, a receipt and one small next task straight away: "Your booking is confirmed, Emma, we're delighted. Your receipt is attached. Next step: when you have your flight details, send them over and we'll arrange your transfer."

## Four root causes of no-shows

| Cause | Sign | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Logistics | No flight booked, no time off | Ask about flights in week one and help |
| Budget | Questions about payment plans, silence | Clarify payment options |
| Trust and fear | Regret messages, new questions | Name the fear, offer a call with the clinician |
| Pressure from others | "My family isn't keen" | Content and a call for the family |

Prevention starts with diagnosing the cause.

## Counting back from treatment day

Plan contact backwards from the date, so the patient feels progress rather than waiting:

- **Week one:** the flight. The most critical threshold; confirm it and arrange the transfer.
- **T-14:** preparation information and a consented story from a similar patient.
- **T-7:** day-by-day schedule and clinician-approved preparation instructions.
- **T-3:** the driver's name and photo, the meeting point, an emergency number.
- **T-1:** a short "see you tomorrow".

If a regret message arrives, don't say "don't worry". Name the concern, normalise it and offer concrete proof or a short call with the clinician. If the patient wants to postpone, set the new date in the same conversation.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much should the deposit be?

That depends on your policy. Enough to signal commitment, not so much that it makes the decision harder, and always deducted from the total.

### What happens if the patient isn't clinically suitable?

Your refund policy should cover this explicitly and protect the patient. An honest "no" from the clinician builds trust.

### Does a deposit solve no-shows on its own?

No. It reduces risk; the real effect comes from communication after booking.
