> **Short answer:** Segment the list first and remove anyone who has opted out or never gave consent. Send the rest a short, personal message with a new reason to talk, not a sales pitch. If there is no reply, send an honest break-up message that takes the pressure off. Every touch needs a genuine reason: new information, real timing or an offer to reassess.

## Why old enquiries are your cheapest patients

Every old enquiry is interest you once paid for with ad spend. These people already know your clinic, were already interested in a treatment and, more often than not, still have the problem. For most clinics, restarting a good conversation with this list is faster and cheaper than buying new leads.

The catch is that old enquiries are scattered: some in the CRM, some on an old WhatsApp phone, some in a spreadsheet belonging to someone who left. The first job is to get them all in one place.

## Step 1: Segment the list

Sending everyone the same message is the quickest way to fail. Split the list into at least these groups:

| Segment | Who | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| A | Got a quote, went quiet | Offer to revisit the plan |
| B | Sent photos or X-rays, never got a quote | Offer to complete the assessment |
| C | Only sent a first message | Reintroduce yourself, short and light |
| D | Gave a date but never came | Ask if that timing still applies |
| E | Had treatment elsewhere | No selling; goodwill and support |
| F | Opted out or never consented | Never contacted |

Segment F is not negotiable. Contacting people who opted out or never consented is unethical and, under UK GDPR and PECR, a legal risk as well.

## Step 2: Find a new reason for every touch

"Hi, are you still interested?" is not a reason. Real reasons are:

- **New information:** a new treatment option, a new clinician, a changed process.
- **Real timing:** a date the patient once gave is approaching, a new year, the end of the holidays.
- **Reassessment:** "You had a quote from us a while ago; things may have changed. If you'd like, we can go through your plan again as it stands today."

The last framing is particularly strong and far more honest than a discount. It does not sell; it offers a service.

## Step 3: Write the reactivation message

A good reactivation message is short, personal and puts no pressure on the patient to reply:

"Hi Emma, it's Dan from the clinic. You spoke to us about implants last spring. It's been a while, so I wondered whether it's still on your mind. If it is, we could go through your plan again as it stands now. If not, no need to reply at all."

No discount, no false urgency, no "last chance".

## Step 4: The break-up message

If several touches get no reply, send an honest break-up message:

"Emma, this is my last message. If it's not on your mind right now, I completely understand and won't keep getting in touch. If you ever need us in future, you can reach me on this number."

It takes the pressure off and, surprisingly often, gets a reply: some say "actually I'm still thinking about it", some give a clear no. Both are useful. But mean it: if you write again a week after a break-up message, your messages lose all weight.

## Step 5: Rhythm and measurement

Reactivation is a rhythm, not a one-off campaign. Every enquiry that drops out of active follow-up can be revisited at three, six and twelve months, each time with a new reason. Measure each round: reply rate, conversations restarted, quotes and treatments. Those numbers tell you which segment and which message work.

Reactivation comes after [quote follow-up](/blog/follow-up-after-sending-treatment-quote); do not confuse the two. Follow-up keeps a warm conversation going; reactivation reopens a cold one.

## Patients who went elsewhere

Segment E matters for reputation, not revenue. Write to them warmly, without criticising the other clinic, and let them know you are there for check-ups or a second opinion. Some come back years later or recommend you to others.

## Frequently asked questions

### How old can an enquiry be and still be worth reactivating?

Enquiries from the last one to two years are usually still valuable. For older records, check that your consent to contact is still valid first.

### Should I offer a discount when reactivating?

Not as your first tool. "Let's go through your plan again as it stands today" is more honest and more effective. A discount signals the first price was not real.

### Will sending messages in bulk put our WhatsApp account at risk?

Yes, unsolicited and impersonal bulk sends can get an account restricted. Use the WhatsApp Business API, approved templates and genuine personalisation.
