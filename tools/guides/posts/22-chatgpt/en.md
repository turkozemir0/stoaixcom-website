> **Short answer:** AI assistants recommend clinics to the extent that they can find consistent, verifiable information about them on the web. To be visible: write service pages that answer the question directly, share concrete details such as price ranges, process and clinician background, keep your name, address and services consistent across Google Business Profile and directories, collect genuine reviews and aim to be mentioned on other sites. This doesn't replace classic SEO; it builds on it.

## What changed

Research no longer stops at a search engine. International patients in particular ask AI assistants questions like "Which city and clinic for implants in Turkey?" and get a single answer with a few clinic names, price ranges and things to watch for.

Those answers aren't random. Assistants draw on web content, reviews, directories and news. The more clear, consistent, verifiable information exists about you, the more likely you are to be recommended. This is called GEO (generative engine optimisation) or AI visibility.

## How it differs from classic SEO

| | Classic SEO | AI visibility |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Top search ranking | Being named in the AI answer |
| Outcome | A clicked link | A read answer, often with no click |
| Strong signals | Keywords, backlinks, technical health | Clear answers, concrete facts, consistent sources |
| Content shape | Long pages | Sections that answer in the first sentence |

They aren't rivals. Most assistants rely on pages search engines have found, so a technically healthy, well-ranked site wins in both.

## What to do

### 1. Answer in the first sentence

Open service pages with the questions patients actually ask: "How long do zirconia crowns take?", "When can I fly after implants?" Give the answer in the first two sentences, then the detail. Assistants quote this structure easily.

### 2. Share concrete facts

"Affordable prices, experienced team" says nothing. Price ranges or the factors that set them, treatment length, how many days to stay, clinicians' specialisms and experience, guarantee terms: these are quotable.

### 3. The same facts everywhere

Your name, address, phone and services should match across your website, Google Business Profile, social media and directories. Contradictions make it harder for an assistant to recommend you confidently.

### 4. Genuine reviews and mentions

Assistants weigh what others say about you more than what you say about yourself. Steady genuine reviews, mentions in industry or local press and clinician interviews matter. See [when to ask patients for reviews](/blog/when-to-ask-patients-for-reviews).

### 5. Structured data

Add structured data (schema) for organisation, clinicians, services and FAQs. It helps search engines and assistants read your information correctly.

## How to measure it

There's no standard metric yet. A simple method: once a month, ask ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity 10–20 questions your patients ask and record whether you're named and with what information. Add "AI assistant" as an option to "How did you find us?"

Keep within healthcare advertising rules: content should inform, not make bold promises.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pay ChatGPT to recommend my clinic?

No. These answers don't sell ad slots in the classic sense. Visibility is earned through your content and reputation.

### Should I stop doing classic SEO?

No. Most assistants use pages indexed by search engines; classic SEO is still the foundation.

### How long until I see results?

There's no fixed timeline. Visibility grows as content, reviews and mentions build up; track it monthly.
