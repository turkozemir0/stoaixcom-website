> **Short answer:** Split follow-up into three phases: the first 72 hours are warm (frequent and short), days 4–14 are lukewarm (spaced out, each touch bringing something new), days 15–90 are nurture (once or twice a month, informative). The schedule pauses when the patient replies and stops when they say no. No follow-up message is ever just "any update?".

## What does silence after a quote mean?

For most clinics, silence means "they didn't like the price". In practice it is usually one of four things: the patient is digesting the quote, showing it to a partner or family, comparing it with others, or sitting on a question they feel awkward asking. None of those is a no.

A quote that is never followed up, though, becomes a definite no. Turning a warm enquiry into a patient is a much shorter road than finding a new one, and every quote left to go cold is ad budget that was paid for and never used.

## How to send the quote

Follow-up starts with the quote itself. A good quote message has four parts:

1. **A personal opener:** refer to something the patient said on the call.
2. **A three-line summary:** what will be done, how long it takes, the total.
3. **The detail:** a PDF or link.
4. **One next step:** "Shall we go through it together on a 10-minute call tomorrow?"

A 45–90 second voice note or video after the quote puts a human face beside the number. Text carries information; voice carries trust.

## The three-phase schedule

| Phase | Timing | Frequency | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm | 0–72 hours | 2–4 touches | Catch questions, read the quote together |
| Lukewarm | Days 4–14 | 2–3 touches | Find the real concern, involve the partner |
| Nurture | Days 15–90 | 1–2 a month | Keep the door open until the timing is right |

Beyond 90 days it is no longer follow-up but reactivation, which we cover in our [guide to reactivating old enquiries](/blog/reactivating-old-patient-enquiries).

**Warm phase:** write little but write well. One question two to four hours after the quote ("Does the timeline in the plan work for you?"), then a value-adding message the next morning, in the patient's time zone.

**Lukewarm phase:** match the content to the patient's main concern. A short note from the clinician for someone worried about trust; a day-by-day itinerary for someone worried about logistics; a plain summary their partner can read for someone who needs to talk it over at home. Around day 14, send a message that gives them permission to say "not now": "If now isn't the right time, just let me know and I won't keep chasing." It takes the pressure off and, surprisingly often, gets a reply.

**Nurture phase:** send information, not sales. A common question, a detail about recovery, a genuine change in availability. New-start moments such as the new year or the end of the holidays are natural reasons to get in touch.

## How to write a follow-up that adds value

"Any update?" gives the patient a task and nothing else. A good follow-up has a personal hook, one piece of value, a micro-question or a "no need to reply" line, and a short, human tone.

Things you can offer as value:

- The question similar patients ask most, with the answer
- A two-line personal note from the clinician
- Practical details about travel, accommodation or recovery days
- A simple comparison table of the options
- A plain-language summary written for the partner or family
- Real context: a new date in the clinician's diary, a change in exchange rates

The last one matters: context is never invented. If "only two slots left" is not true, it is not written. We explain why false urgency backfires in our [guide to ethical persuasion](/blog/ethical-persuasion-clinic-sales).

## Channel and timing

Mix channels but respect preferences: a WhatsApp message, a voice note, a scheduled call. With international patients, always think in their local time.

## When does follow-up stop?

- When the patient replies, the schedule pauses; if the conversation goes quiet again, it restarts from that day.
- When the patient clearly says no or asks you not to contact them, it stops. That is both ethics and UK GDPR.
- If the patient had treatment elsewhere, keep the relationship warm and wish them well. Never criticise the other clinic.

## Frequently asked questions

### How many times should I follow up after a quote?

Two to four touches in the first 72 hours, two or three over the next ten days, then once or twice a month is a sensible frame. What matters more than the count is that every touch brings something new.

### When should I give up on a patient who never replies?

If nothing has come back after 90 days, end active follow-up and move them to a reactivation list. If they say no, stop immediately.

### Should I offer a discount in follow-up?

Not for nothing. Silence is rarely about price; find the real concern first. If budget is genuinely the barrier, talk about changing the scope.

### Does automating follow-up annoy patients?

Information and reminders can be automated; judgement and emotion should stay human. When the patient replies, the automated sequence should stop and a person should take over.
