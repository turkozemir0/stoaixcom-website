> **Short answer:** Text carries information, voice notes carry warmth, video carries trust, and an unannounced call often carries irritation. Prices and clinical information should always be in writing. Keep voice notes under a minute, on one topic, followed by a one-line written summary. Book phone calls in advance at a time that suits the patient's time zone. Moving between channels is a ladder, climbed one step at a time with the patient's agreement.

## The channel is part of the message

Clinics usually choose channels by habit: reception likes the phone, the sales team likes WhatsApp. For the patient, the channel sets the tone.

| Channel | What it carries | Best for | Watch out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text message | Information, permanence | Prices, schedules, preparation | Long texts go unread |
| Voice note | Interest, warmth | A personal welcome, a short explanation | No prices or clinical details by voice |
| Short video | Trust, "a real person" | Introduction before a first call, a clinic tour | Must not feel like an ad |
| Phone call | Speed, depth | Booked conversations, complex questions | Unannounced calls irritate |
| Video call | The highest trust | Meeting the clinician, presenting a plan | Must be scheduled |

## Voice note rules

1. **Match or ask.** If the patient sends voice notes, replying in kind is natural. Ask first if they never do.
2. **Under a minute.** Long voice notes go unheard or half-heard.
3. **One topic.**
4. **A written summary.** Follow with a one-line text so nothing is lost if they can't listen.
5. **Prices and clinical information stay in writing.**

## Short personal video

A 30-second personal video before the first call builds the "real person" feeling in one go. Show your face, use the patient's name and say what happens next. Sincerity beats production values.

## Phone calls

The phone is the fastest way to resolve something complex, but many patients, especially international ones, find unannounced calls intrusive. Offer times in writing first ("Tomorrow at 2pm or 5pm, which suits you?"), respect their time zone and daily rhythm, and send a written summary afterwards.

Missed inbound calls are a separate problem: an unanswered patient calls the next clinic. Answering incoming calls matters as much as planning outgoing ones.

## The channel ladder

Text, voice note, short video, booked call, video call: each step needs the patient's agreement and a reason. "This would take a while to explain by message; shall we do a five-minute call tomorrow?" Skipping steps, such as calling unannounced after the first message, damages trust.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why not give prices in a voice note?

Spoken numbers are easily forgotten or misremembered, and the patient struggles to pass them on to family. Written prices give clarity and trust.

### When should I call an international patient?

In their time zone, during working hours, at a time agreed in writing. Evening and weekend calls only if the patient asks.

### Do I need professional equipment for video messages?

No. A short phone video in a quiet, well-lit place is enough.
